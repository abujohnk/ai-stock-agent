🇮🇳 India Deep Dive

## Indian AI/Tech Sector Analysis

The Indian AI and technology sector presents a bifurcated picture. Smaller, agile players are decisively outpacing legacy giants, with mid-cap names averaging revenue growth well above 20% versus the 3-5% range plaguing TCS, Infosys, and Wipro. The sector's composite scores reveal meaningful dispersion, suggesting stock selection matters enormously here rather than broad index exposure.

Mid-cap IT and electronics manufacturing pure-plays are clearly leading. Dixon Technologies (25.77% revenue growth), Persistent Systems (23.53%), and Kaynes Technology (33.24%) demonstrate that hardware-linked AI infrastructure and digital engineering services are capturing disproportionate growth. Meanwhile, large-cap stalwarts like Infosys and TCS score below 50, weighed down by single-digit revenue growth despite healthy margins, reflecting client spending caution in their core enterprise segments.

Oracle Financial Services stands categorically apart, earning the sector's sole Strong Buy with a composite score of 86.0. Its combination of 39.38% net margins, 47.41% ROE, and 12.05% revenue growth reflects an extraordinarily capital-efficient business model — one that generates exceptional shareholder returns without needing aggressive top-line expansion. This profitability profile is virtually unmatched across the peer group, making OFSS the standout holding for quality-conscious investors seeking durable compounding in Indian technology.

Snowflake's negative margins make its Watch designation unambiguous.

⚖️ India vs Global Comparison

## India vs Global: Head-to-Head

Indian AI stocks trail meaningfully on most financial metrics — a 54.5 average composite score versus 67.7 globally, thinner net margins (14.3% vs 27.0%), and revenue growth roughly half the global pace (12.4% vs 25.7%). Even the standout performers reflect this gap: Oracle Financial Services leads India at 86.0, while Palantir tops globally at 88.8. Where India holds genuine ground is profitability structure — a 23.2% average ROE isn't embarrassing against the global 30.6%, suggesting Indian players convert equity reasonably efficiently despite lower margins.

For investors, the choice hinges on risk appetite. Global AI stocks offer superior growth and margin compounding, making them stronger candidates for pure-play AI exposure. Indian stocks, however, offer currency diversification, domestic demand tailwinds, and relatively lower valuations. A blended allocation — anchored in global leaders but with selective Indian positions like Oracle Financial Services — arguably captures both the AI growth wave and emerging-market optionality.

# Top Stock Picks

Palantir (PLTR)

Palantir earns its #1 ranking through a rare convergence of hypergrowth and profitability, sustaining 56% revenue growth while delivering a 50% net margin — an almost unheard-of combination at this scale. Its most impressive metric is the 3-year price CAGR of 92%, reflecting genuine market recognition of durable AI platform value. *Invest for compounding AI infrastructure exposure, accepting premium valuation as the cost of entry.*

NVIDIA (NVDA)

NVIDIA's dominance in AI accelerator hardware drives its #2 position, underpinned by an extraordinary 117% ROE that signals unmatched capital efficiency across the semiconductor sector. At just 20x earnings against 65% revenue growth, it remains the most compelling value-to-quality trade in the AI universe. *Core holding for investors seeking leveraged exposure to the AI infrastructure buildout.*

Oracle Financial Services (OFSS.NS)

OFSS earns its ranking as a quiet compounder, combining a 47% ROE with a 39% net margin inside a stable, recurring-revenue banking software model. Its 40.8% 3-year price CAGR validates sustained institutional recognition of this quality at a reasonable 24x earnings. *A high-quality, lower-volatility compounder for investors prioritizing profitability over growth velocity.*

Alphabet (GOOGL)

Alphabet's 50% net margin at its revenue scale is the standout metric, reflecting Search's entrenched monetization paired with accelerating Cloud and AI contribution. At 15x earnings, the valuation disconnect versus peers makes this among the highest-conviction value opportunities in mega-cap tech. *Buy quality at a discount with AI optionality included.*

Meta Platforms (META)

Meta's 32% ROE and 22% revenue growth at its scale demonstrate that its advertising flywheel remains structurally intact despite heavy AI and Reality Labs investment. At 21.5x earnings, the market undervalues the durability of that earnings engine. *Compelling entry for investors seeking profitable, AI-enhanced growth at a fair price.*

⚠️ Risk Factors

## Risk Factors

Wipro and Snowflake both carry meaningful execution risk — Wipro faces margin pressure amid sluggish IT spending and weak deal momentum, while Snowflake's premium valuation leaves little room for error as revenue growth decelerates. Cyient's Avoid rating reflects deeper concerns: deteriorating earnings visibility and exposure to cyclical end-markets that amplify downside risk in a slowdown.

Sector-wide, AI stocks face a convergence of headwinds that investors shouldn't dismiss. Valuations across the AI landscape remain stretched, with many names priced for perfection despite uncertain monetisation timelines. Regulatory scrutiny is intensifying in both the EU and India, adding compliance costs and operational uncertainty. Perhaps most structurally threatening is AI commoditisation — as foundation models become cheaper and more accessible, pricing power for AI-adjacent services erodes. A stronger dollar and persistent macro tightening further compress emerging-market earnings, hitting Indian IT names particularly hard.
