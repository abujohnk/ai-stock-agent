## Executive Summary

As of November 2026, AI-driven technology stocks remain one of the most compelling long-term growth narratives in global equity markets, yet performance and quality are increasingly diverging across geographies and business models. Across our 28-stock universe, composite scores range dramatically, with only four names earning a Strong Buy designation and eleven consigned to Hold — a distribution reflecting a maturing cycle where execution discipline now separates winners from the field.

The India-Global gap is striking and strategically significant. Global AI leaders average a composite score of 67.4 versus 46.8 for Indian technology names, underscoring that while Indian IT commands structural tailwinds, it has yet to translate AI adoption into the margin expansion and capital efficiency that defines world-class operators. This gap represents both a risk and a rerating opportunity for select Indian names demonstrating credible AI monetization roadmaps.

The investment thesis remains concentrated at the top. Palantir (88.8), NVIDIA (86.5), Alphabet (78.5), and Meta (78.5) collectively exemplify the rare convergence of explosive revenue growth, elite net margins exceeding 31%, and strong returns on equity. For investors navigating this universe, conviction should remain anchored in this proven global cohort while monitoring Indian challengers for inflection signals.

🇮🇳 India Deep Dive

## Indian AI/Tech Sector Analysis

The Indian AI/IT sector presents a bifurcated picture. Aggregate revenue growth remains healthy, with several mid-caps exceeding 20% growth, while large-cap stalwarts TCS and Infosys languish below 5%, suggesting structural demand softness in traditional IT outsourcing. Net margins remain broadly respectable, though Dixon Technologies' razor-thin 2.65% highlights hardware's inherent margin compression.

Mid-cap pure-play technology names are demonstrably outperforming. Persistent Systems (23.5% revenue growth), Affle India (19.6%), and Intellect Design Arena (21.5%) demonstrate that focused, niche-oriented businesses are capturing disproportionate demand tailwinds from digital transformation and AI adoption. Large-cap IT, by contrast, faces pricing headwinds and client budget scrutiny, reflected in TCS and Wipro's uninspiring sub-5% and sub-4% growth respectively. Semiconductor-adjacent Kaynes Technology shows explosive 33% revenue growth, though its 5.6% ROE warrants monitoring.

Oracle Financial Services stands definitively apart, combining 12% revenue growth with an extraordinary 39.4% net margin and 47.4% ROE — a composite score of 69 that leads the sector by a substantial margin. This financial technology specialist benefits from mission-critical banking software lock-in, pricing power, and an asset-light model, making it the sector's most compelling risk-adjusted investment case among all 16 names screened.

⚖️ India vs Global Comparison

## India vs. Global AI: Head-to-Head

Indian AI stocks trail meaningfully on nearly every metric, averaging a composite score of 46.8 against the global cohort's 67.4. The gap is sharpest in profitability — global players average 26.9% net margins versus India's 14.2% — and revenue growth, where globals nearly double India's 12.4% with a 25.7% clip. Even the best India can offer, Oracle Financial Services at 69.0, falls well short of Palantir's 88.8. India's genuine edge lies in return on equity — 22.1% is respectable — and relative valuation, where domestic names carry less speculative froth.

For investors, the choice is essentially growth versus stability. Global AI exposure, led by names like Palantir, offers superior compounding potential but demands tolerance for elevated valuations and volatility. Indian AI stocks suit investors seeking steadier, domestically-anchored businesses with solid capital efficiency. A blended allocation captures both the high-octane global AI cycle and India's durable, margin-conscious IT franchise.

⚠️ Risk Factors

Several holdings warrant caution. Cyient's score of 17.0 demands serious scrutiny, suggesting deteriorating fundamentals across multiple dimensions. Wipro at 38.2 reflects ongoing struggles with revenue growth and margin pressure that have persistently plagued large-cap Indian IT. Snowflake's 33.5 score highlights the tension between its premium valuation and slowing growth, while Kaynes and Newgen face execution risk as smaller players in competitive, capital-intensive segments.

Sector-wide risks compound these individual concerns. Indian IT faces rupee volatility, wage inflation, and client budget scrutiny in key Western markets. Globally, AI commoditisation is accelerating — as foundation models become utilities, pricing power erodes for middleware and services players. Regulatory headwinds around data sovereignty and AI governance are tightening in both the EU and India. Meanwhile, stretched valuations across the AI thematic leave little margin for error; any earnings disappointment risks sharp de-ratings, particularly in momentum-driven names.
