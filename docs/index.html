## Executive Summary

Five stocks carry Strong Buy ratings, led by Palantir, NVIDIA, and Oracle Financial Services, posting composite scores above 86 and revenue growth ranging from 12% to 65%, underscoring the breadth of monetization across the AI stack.

The gap between global and Indian AI stocks — 67.7 versus 54.6 on a 100-point composite — reveals a meaningful maturity differential. The central investment takeaway is one of selective conviction. The top five global names offer rare combinations of hypergrowth and profitability that justify continued overweight positioning, while Indian AI stocks warrant patient accumulation as their monetization curves steepen. With 14 stocks rated Buy or better, this universe rewards active management and disciplined stock selection over passive, index-level exposure.

🇮🇳 India Deep Dive

## Indian AI/Tech Sector Analysis

The Indian AI/IT sector presents a bifurcated picture: high-growth mid-caps are decisively outpacing legacy large-caps. Revenue growth clusters sharply between emerging players (Kaynes at 33.24%, Dixon at 25.77%, Persistent at 23.53%) and stagnating giants (TCS at 4.58%, Infosys at 4.57%, Wipro at 3.97%), suggesting structural momentum has shifted downstream. Cyient's negative revenue growth and 4.6% net margin signals genuine distress within engineering services.

Mid-cap and semiconductor-adjacent names are clearly dominating. Dixon Technologies and Kaynes Technology reflect India's hardware/EMS manufacturing renaissance, riding PLI tailwinds with exceptional revenue acceleration. Meanwhile, Persistent Systems demonstrates that mid-cap IT services can combine growth with respectable 10.73% margins and 22.92% ROE, outscoring every large-cap IT name on composite metrics. Large-caps face the dual headwind of enterprise spending caution and commoditisation pressure.

Oracle Financial Services stands categorically apart, scoring 86.0 against a field where the next competitor trails at 71.0. Its combination of 39.38% net margins, 47.41% ROE, and 12.05% revenue growth represents a rare, capital-light compounding machine. The business benefits from deep BFSI moats, recurring licensing revenues, and minimal capital requirements — qualities that make it the sector's most exceptional risk-adjusted opportunity.

🌐 Global AI Leaders

## Global AI Equity Analysis

The dominant theme across top-ranked AI stocks is the convergence of explosive revenue growth with exceptional profitability — a rare combination that separates genuine AI beneficiaries from narrative-driven names. Palantir leads the composite rankings at 88.8, posting 56% revenue growth alongside a 50% net margin, while NVIDIA's 86.5 score reflects arguably the most extraordinary financial profile in technology: 65% revenue growth paired with 63% net margins and a staggering 117% ROE.

The Leaders segment — Palantir, NVIDIA, Alphabet, and Meta — demonstrates the most consistent strength, with all four earning Strong Buy ratings and each sustaining net margins above 31%. This cohort benefits from proprietary competitive moats that convert AI investment cycles into durable cash generation rather than transient revenue spikes.

The most notable surprise is Amazon's relatively modest 62.8 score, underwhelming given its AWS dominance and market reputation as a core AI infrastructure provider. Its 12% revenue growth and 17% net margin suggest the market's AI enthusiasm around AWS may exceed current financial reality. Conversely, Snowflake's 33.5 Watch rating validates skepticism toward profitability-absent growth stories, with -20% net margins and -48% ROE making its AI credentials financially unsustainable at present.

⚖️ India vs Global Comparison

## India vs. Global AI: Head-to-Head

Indian AI stocks trail meaningfully on most financial metrics — a 54.6 average composite score versus 67.7 globally, with net margins of 14.3% less than half the global cohort's 27.0%. Revenue growth tells a similar story: 12.4% domestically against a striking 25.7% globally. Where India pushes back is capital efficiency — a 23.8% average ROE is respectable, narrowing the gap against the global field's 30.6%. Oracle Financial Services (86.0) nearly matches Palantir (88.8) at the individual stock level, proving India can produce world-class AI names.

For investors, the choice hinges on risk appetite. Global AI exposure — particularly U.S.-listed names — offers superior growth velocity and margins, but commands premium valuations. Indian AI stocks offer a domestically-driven growth runway, stronger currency-risk insulation, and tighter valuation support. A blended allocation captures both the high-octane global compounders and India's under-appreciated, capital-efficient operators.

⚠️ Risk Factors

## Risk Factors

The weaker names in our universe expose tangible vulnerabilities. Cyient's Avoid rating (23.0/100) reflects deteriorating fundamentals and execution concerns that go beyond cyclical softness. Snowflake (33.5/100) remains unprofitable while facing intensifying competition from cloud hyperscalers building equivalent data infrastructure natively. Wipro's Watch rating signals stagnating growth and margin pressure as clients scrutinise discretionary IT spending.

Broader sector risks compound these stock-specific concerns. AI valuations remain stretched, leaving little margin for earnings disappointment. Regulatory headwinds are accelerating — the EU AI Act, emerging Indian data localisation rules, and potential US export controls all threaten operating models. Perhaps most critically, AI commoditisation is real: as foundation models become utilities, pricing power erodes across the stack. Macroeconomically, a stronger rupee, sluggish US enterprise budgets, and rising interest rates continue suppressing the multiple investors are willing to pay for growth-dependent AI names.
