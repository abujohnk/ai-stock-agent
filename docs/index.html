🇮🇳 India Deep Dive

## Indian AI/Tech Sector Analysis

The Indian AI/IT sector presents a bifurcated picture. Established large-caps like TCS and Infosys show anaemic revenue growth of 4.58% and 4.57% respectively, reflecting global IT spending caution, while mid-cap and pure-play names demonstrate considerably more dynamism, suggesting structural share gains rather than cyclical recovery.

Mid-cap and pure-play segments are clearly outperforming. Kaynes Technology leads revenue growth at 33.24%, while Persistent Systems delivers an impressive 23.53% alongside a healthy 10.73% margin, indicating disciplined scaling. Affle India's 19.56% growth with 16.85% net margins confirms digital advertising's resilience. Meanwhile, large-cap IT — Wipro's 3.97% growth and Cyient's negative 1.25% — signals meaningful competitive pressure at scale.

Oracle Financial Services is the undisputed standout, posting a composite score of 86.0 — seventeen points clear of second-ranked Persistent. Its combination of 39.38% net margins, 47.41% ROE, and 12.05% revenue growth reflects an extraordinarily efficient, high-moat business serving financial institutions with mission-critical software. Unlike growth-heavy peers burning margin for expansion, OFSS compounds quietly with exceptional capital efficiency, making it arguably the highest-quality franchise in the entire Indian technology universe.

⚖️ India vs Global Comparison

## India vs. Global AI: Head-to-Head

Indian AI stocks trail their global peers on nearly every quantitative measure, yet the gap is narrower than headline scores suggest. The average composite score of 53.0 versus 68.9 reflects a real profitability gulf — global companies post net margins of 27.3% against India's 14.4%, and revenue growth of 25.7% nearly doubles India's 12.4%. ROE tells a more competitive story: India's 26.1% versus 31.0% globally is a meaningful but not insurmountable gap. Even India's top performer, Oracle Financial Services at 86.0, barely trails NVIDIA's 86.5 — proof that best-in-class Indian names are genuinely world-competitive.

For investors, the choice hinges on risk appetite. Global exposure, led by high-growth infrastructure plays, offers superior earnings momentum today. Indian AI stocks, however, provide rupee-denominated stability, strong ROE fundamentals, and significant domestic digitisation tailwinds. A blended allocation captures both the explosive growth of global AI infrastructure and the compounding, lower-volatility returns emerging from India's expanding technology ecosystem.

⚠️ Risk Factors

## Risk Factors

Wipro and Snowflake's middling scores reflect genuine structural concerns: Wipro faces margin pressure and sluggish discretionary IT spending from Western clients, while Snowflake's growth-at-any-cost model leaves it exposed when revenue momentum slows. Cyient's Avoid rating is unambiguous — deteriorating fundamentals and weak earnings visibility make it difficult to justify at current levels.

Broader sector risks are substantial. AI valuations remain stretched, with markets pricing perfection into companies still navigating uncertain monetisation paths. Commoditisation is accelerating — as foundation models become cheaper and more accessible, differentiation erodes and pricing power weakens. Indian IT services firms face structural disruption if AI meaningfully compresses software development headcount. Regulatory pressure is tightening across the EU and increasingly in the US. Meanwhile, a stronger rupee, potential US recession, and rising interest rates all create macro headwinds. Investors should demand genuine earnings evidence, not narrative.
